Entry: one funny line, written by `main.ts` to `/output/entry.txt`.

## Final line (chosen)

> My phone's autocorrect changed 'I owe you' to 'I love you' in a text to my landlord. Now I owe him rent and an awkward hug.

123 characters, one line, under the 200 character gate.

## Candidates and scoring (judge criteria, in order)

1. A funny one-line joke a stranger gets in one read.
2. Surprises: sets up an expectation, then breaks it.
3. Tight: every word earns its place, the laugh lands at the end.
4. Fresh: not a stock joke, not a pun everyone has heard.
5. Reads cleanly out loud, no reference that needs context.

Candidates considered:

- Chosen: the autocorrect "I owe you" to "I love you" (123 chars). A
  relatable phone mishap, the landlord makes the stakes real, and the
  punch is a clean reversal in the last words: rent plus an awkward hug.
- "My phone's autocorrect turned 'I owe you' into 'I love you' in a text
  to my landlord, so now I owe him rent and an awkward hug." (127 chars).
  First draft of the chosen line, looser: one long clause where the
  revision splits the punch into its own sentence.
- "Autocorrect changed 'I owe you' to 'I love you' in a text to my
  landlord, so now I owe him rent and an awkward hug." (107 chars).
  Shorter, but dropping "My phone's" loses the everyday setup that makes
  the mishap feel real.

Angles deliberately avoided: the brief's own examples are generic, so the
line leans on one specific, concrete mishap instead of a stock pun.

## Run

```sh
node main.ts
```
